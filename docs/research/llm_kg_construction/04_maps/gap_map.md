# Gap Map

## G1. Educational constructs are not ordinary entities

Most KGC papers score factual triples. KnowAct nodes are constructs that must be source-faithful, stable in granularity, and diagnosable through interaction.

## G2. Grounding and measurement validity are conflated

A textbook can support a concept definition without supporting a six-level mastery rubric. Provenance classes must separate extracted facts, entailments, and pedagogical extensions.

## G3. Recall is under-measured

Reviewing only model proposals measures precision more easily than missed concepts. Independent expert annotation is needed before candidate review.

## G4. Agent count is mistaken for method quality

Specialized roles are useful when they create decision independence or enforce a contract. No reviewed work proves that an arbitrary increase in agents improves graph validity.

## G5. Operational reliability is rarely a first-class metric

Timeouts, malformed JSON, stale source caches, partial checkpoints, and resume errors can corrupt a graph even when semantic prompts are strong.

## G6. Downstream validity is missing

Intrinsic graph quality should be connected to KnowAct outcomes: expert review burden, simulator fidelity, and stability of tested-agent rankings.
