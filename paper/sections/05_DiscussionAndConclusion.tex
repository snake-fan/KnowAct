\section{Discussion and Conclusion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{What \knowact Measures}

Low reconstruction error is not evidence of functional ToM when it can be obtained through leakage or a fixed prior. \knowact therefore pairs endpoint accuracy with an observable diagnostic process.

The visibility boundary limits available evidence, questions consume a fixed budget, map updates cite visible turns, and non-adaptive agents provide controls. Together, these constraints distinguish:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.7em]
    \item \emph{state inference}: interpreting what an answer implies;
    \item \emph{state maintenance}: integrating evidence over time;
    \item \emph{state-conditioned action}: using the current map to select a probe.
\end{enumerate}

The interaction is a form of active information acquisition over a structured latent state. Unlike pool-based active learning, actions are open-ended questions interpreted by a conversational simulator.

Unlike conventional adaptive testing, the probe set need not be fixed or calibrated in advance. This flexibility motivates the benchmark while making grounding, evidence audit, and simulator validation essential.

The benchmark is diagnostic rather than task-complete. It does not yet test whether a reconstructed map improves teaching, recommendation, or collaboration.

\subsection{Limitations and Threats to Validity}

\paragraph{Simulator fidelity.}
A simulator can be consistent with a reviewed map without matching how people express partial knowledge. Human studies must test response similarity, diagnostic value, and transfer of agent rankings.

\paragraph{Construction circularity.}
Related model families may author artifacts, simulate users, and serve as tested agents. Correlated biases could make episodes easier or distort rankings.

Source grounding, human review, model-family diversity, rule-based fixtures, and matched human validation reduce this risk but cannot eliminate it.

\paragraph{Simplified knowledge state.}
A static L0--L5 state cannot capture context dependence, procedural fluency, forgetting, confidence, or learning during interaction.

The initial benchmark freezes the hidden state to make reconstruction identifiable. Dynamic learning and state transition are important extensions.

\paragraph{Initial domain scope.}
The first release focuses on statistical learning. Evaluation across domains is required to test transfer across graph topologies, source styles, and concept distributions.

\paragraph{Operational claims.}
Success demonstrates behavior compatible with structured user modeling under benchmark constraints. It does not establish human-like beliefs, consciousness, or a psychologically faithful ToM mechanism.

\subsection{Conclusion and Outlook}

We introduced \knowact, a benchmark for evaluating functional ToM through active knowledge-state diagnosis.

A tested agent sees a reviewed domain graph but not the user-specific map. Under a fixed budget, it must ask diagnostic questions, maintain an evidence-backed working map, and reconstruct the hidden state.

A visibility-separated simulator and deterministic scoring protocol make the process auditable. The design isolates active user-state acquisition from broader social or task success.

The next step is empirical validation across adaptive and non-adaptive agents, simulator designs, matched human episodes, and additional knowledge domains.
